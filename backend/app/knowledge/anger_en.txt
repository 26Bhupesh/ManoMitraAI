[CONTEXT]
Anger can arise when you feel hurt, misunderstood, treated unfairly, or overwhelmed. It often shows up as tension in the body, racing thoughts, or a strong urge to react.

[VALIDATION]
Feeling angry does not make you a bad person. Anger is a human emotion that often signals something important needs attention.

[TECHNIQUE]
Pause before reacting. Try unclenching your jaw, lowering your shoulders, and taking one slow breath in and out.

[GUIDANCE]
You do not have to solve anything immediately. Creating a small pause can prevent the situation from escalating.

[REFLECTION]
What did you notice in your body when you slowed down for a moment?
---

[CONTEXT]
Anger can build when emotions stay unexpressed or ignored over time.

[VALIDATION]
Holding everything inside can be exhausting. Your feelings deserve to be acknowledged.

[TECHNIQUE]
If it feels safe, try writing down what you are angry about without filtering or judging your words.

[GUIDANCE]
Writing is not about blaming. It is about understanding what is underneath the anger.

[REFLECTION]
Was there a deeper feeling beneath the anger, such as hurt, fear, or frustration?
---

[CONTEXT]
During intense anger, the body can enter a heightened state, making it hard to think clearly.

[VALIDATION]
This reaction is not a personal failure. It is your nervous system responding to stress.

[TECHNIQUE]
Engage your body to release tension. This could be a short walk, stretching your arms, or pressing your feet firmly into the ground.

[GUIDANCE]
Physical movement can help the body calm before the mind follows.

[REFLECTION]
Did your anger change even slightly after moving your body?
---

[CONTEXT]
After anger passes, people sometimes feel guilt or regret about how they reacted.

[VALIDATION]
Feeling regret shows awareness, not weakness. It means you care about your actions and their impact.

[TECHNIQUE]
Offer yourself a moment of self-compassion. Remind yourself that learning to respond differently takes time.

[GUIDANCE]
Growth comes from reflection, not self-punishment.

[REFLECTION]
If this situation happened again, what is one small thing you might try differently?
