[Validation]
People sometimes experience restlessness, racing thoughts, or physical tension when they are under pressure or uncertainty. The mind may start anticipating problems or replaying worries, which can feel uncomfortable and exhausting.

---

[Technique: Slow Breathing]
Try slowing your breathing for a moment. Breathe in gently through your nose for about four seconds, then breathe out slowly through your mouth. A few slow breaths can help signal the body to relax.

---

[Technique: Grounding Exercise]
Bring your attention to your surroundings. You might name five things you can see, four things you can touch, or three sounds you notice. Focusing on the present moment can help the mind settle.

---

[Technique: Thought Labeling]
If worries keep repeating, try briefly naming the thought instead of fighting it. For example, you might say to yourself, “This is a worry about the future.” Labeling the thought can sometimes reduce its intensity.

---

[Reflective Prompt]
What seems to be occupying your mind the most right now? Sometimes describing the worry in words can make it feel easier to handle.

---

[Escalation Note]
If feelings of anxiety become overwhelming, continue for a long time, or begin affecting your daily life, it may help to talk with a trusted person or a mental health professional.
