[CONTEXT]
Anxiety is a common response to stress, uncertainty, or perceived danger. It can show up as racing thoughts, a fast heartbeat, tightness in the chest, restlessness, or a sense that something bad might happen.

[VALIDATION]
Feeling anxious does not mean something is wrong with you. Many people experience anxiety, especially during challenging or unfamiliar situations. These reactions are your body’s way of trying to protect you.

[TECHNIQUE]
Slow breathing can help calm the body’s stress response. Try breathing in through your nose for 4 seconds, holding for 2 seconds, and breathing out slowly through your mouth for 6 seconds.

[GUIDANCE]
You do not need to force the breath. Just let it be slow and gentle. Even a few cycles can help your body feel slightly more settled.

[REFLECTION]
As you breathe, do you notice any small change in how your body feels?

[CONTEXT]
Anxiety often comes with repeated “what if” thoughts about the future. These thoughts can feel very real, even when there is no immediate danger.

[VALIDATION]
It is understandable to worry when your mind is trying to prepare for problems. This does not mean the thoughts are true or that something bad will happen.

[TECHNIQUE]
Grounding can help bring attention back to the present moment. Try the 5-4-3-2-1 method: name 5 things you can see, 4 things you can feel, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste.

[GUIDANCE]
There is no right or wrong way to do this. Take your time and focus gently on each sense.

[REFLECTION]
Which sense felt easiest to focus on right now?

[CONTEXT]
When anxiety is high, the body can stay tense even after the stressful moment has passed.

[VALIDATION]
Physical tension is a normal part of anxiety. Your body is reacting automatically, not because you chose to feel this way.

[TECHNIQUE]
Try gently tensing and then relaxing your shoulders. Lift them slightly, hold for a few seconds, then let them drop.

[GUIDANCE]
Move slowly and stop if anything feels uncomfortable. The goal is awareness, not forcing relaxation.

[REFLECTION]
How do your shoulders feel compared to before you tried this?
---

[CONTEXT]
Anxiety can make problems feel bigger and harder to manage all at once.

[VALIDATION]
Feeling overwhelmed does not mean you cannot cope. It often means you are dealing with many things at the same time.

[TECHNIQUE]
Break things down into one small step. Write down one worry and then write one small action you can take today, even if it feels minor.

[GUIDANCE]
The action does not need to solve everything. Small steps still count.

[REFLECTION]
What is one small thing you feel able to do right now?
